Welcome to Millbrook

Millbrook is a quiet town of about four thousand people nestled between two low hills in the valley. Most residents have lived here their entire lives, and newcomers are always surprised by how much happens in such a small place.

Main Street runs straight through the center of town. On the east side you will find Hargrove Hardware, a family business that has been selling tools and paint since 1962. Next door is the post office, which doubles as the unofficial community bulletin board. Across the street, Delancey's Diner opens at five in the morning and is always full of farmers by half past five.

Oak Street branches off Main Street to the north and is a quieter stretch lined with maple trees. The first building on Oak Street is a small insurance agency run by a man named Clifford Bates. Then comes a flower shop called Petal & Stem, where the owner grows most of her stock in a greenhouse behind the building. Two doors down is the Golden Loaf, a bakery that has been the heart of Oak Street for nearly thirty years. The Golden Loaf opens before dawn every day. The smell of bread drifts all the way to the corner of Main and Oak on still mornings, and locals claim you can set your watch by the moment the first loaves come out of the oven. At the end of the block there is a small accounting firm that moved in only last year.

Elm Street runs parallel to Oak Street one block west. It is mostly residential, though a veterinary clinic and a small gym occupy the ground floors of two older buildings. The gym is called Iron Valley and caters mostly to the younger crowd, while older residents tend to walk the trail along the creek instead.

The town holds a farmers market every Saturday on the lawn behind the community center. The Golden Loaf always has a table there, selling sourdough, rye, and the cinnamon rolls that reportedly sell out within the first twenty minutes.
